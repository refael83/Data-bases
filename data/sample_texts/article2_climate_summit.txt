TITLE: World Leaders Gather for Emergency Climate Summit in Paris
AUTHOR: Emma Watson
NEWSPAPER: The Guardian
DATE: 2026-04-22
PAGE: 3
TOPIC: World
LANGUAGE: en
---
Heads of state from more than 120 countries convened in Paris on Monday for an emergency climate summit, amid growing alarm over record-breaking temperatures and devastating natural disasters.

The three-day summit, hosted by French President Emmanuel Macron, aims to accelerate global efforts to cut greenhouse gas emissions and mobilize funding for climate adaptation in developing nations.

"The climate crisis is no longer a distant threat. It is here, and it is now," Macron told delegates in his opening address. "We must act with urgency and determination, or face consequences that will be irreversible."

The summit comes on the heels of a summer marked by extreme weather events around the world. Wildfires ravaged parts of Canada and Greece, while deadly floods struck Libya, India, and China. Global average temperatures in July reached the highest levels ever recorded, prompting the United Nations Secretary-General to declare that "the era of global boiling has arrived."

One of the central issues at the summit is climate finance. Developing nations have long argued that wealthy countries, which are historically responsible for the bulk of carbon emissions, should bear a greater share of the cost of addressing climate change.

"We did not cause this crisis, but we are paying the heaviest price," said Kenyan President William Ruto. "Africa contributes less than four percent of global emissions, yet our people are dying from droughts and floods caused by the pollution of others."

A coalition of small island nations presented a proposal calling for a new international fund to compensate countries suffering loss and damage from climate change. The fund, which would require contributions from the worlds largest emitters, has the backing of over 60 countries but faces resistance from the United States and China.

On the emissions front, several countries announced new commitments. Brazil pledged to end illegal deforestation in the Amazon by 2028, while India announced a plan to triple its renewable energy capacity by 2030. The European Union reaffirmed its goal of achieving net-zero emissions by 2050.

Environmental activists, however, expressed skepticism about whether the summit would produce meaningful results. "We have heard these promises before," said Greta Thunberg, speaking at a rally outside the conference venue. "What we need is action, not words."

Scientists warn that the window for limiting global warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels is rapidly closing. According to the latest report from the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change, global emissions must be cut by 43 percent by 2030 to stay on track.

The summit is expected to conclude on Wednesday with a joint declaration outlining concrete steps to address the climate emergency. Whether those steps will be sufficient remains to be seen.
